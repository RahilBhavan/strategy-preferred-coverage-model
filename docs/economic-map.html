Every financing decision has three tests.

Strategy raises capital through securities and allocates it across bitcoin, cash and obligations. To understand an action, trace its effect on all three tests.

1. Per share

What changes in net bitcoin per diluted share under the stated definition?

2. Cash

What must be paid, when, and from which available source?

3. Value

Does the risk-adjusted benefit exceed the full cost and the next-best alternative?

A higher per-share metric can coexist with less cash. A cash payment can be funded while dilution rises. Neither result alone establishes investment value.

The break-even is a one-year result under stated-claim accounting, not a perpetual annual hurdle or fair-value estimate. Market value, taxes, fees, discount rates and payment timing need a separate valuation.

The 12-unit learning route

Use the existing textbook as evidence and examples. Follow this sequence to connect the mechanisms.

1. Bitcoin and dollars

Scarcity, custody and price formation. Why dollar payments still need dollar cash.

2. Why a company wrapper?

Compare direct BTC, a fund, common and preferreds. Identify the exposure each buyer wants.

3. Assets and claims

Reconcile assets, liabilities, preferred rights, common shares and cash flows.

4. Every security's trade

Net proceeds, priority, currency, coupon, conversion, puts and redemption terms.

5. Issuance and dilution

Define the metric, share denominator and use of proceeds before calculating accretion.

6. Full financing cost

Separate cash carry, issue discounts, option cost, terminal claims and investment return.

7. Dated liquidity

Track cash, demand for new securities and principal payments quarter by quarter.

8. The demand loop

Trace how prices affect financing and financing affects the metrics investors observe.

9. Management's choices

Compare BTC purchases, cash retention, issuance, repayments and repurchases on the same date.

10. Business economics

Separate software cash generation from the economics of security issuance and distribution.

11. Control and constraints

Voting, incentives, custody, legal rights, taxes and currency exposure.

12. Decision reconstruction

Explain one historical choice and one adverse case against explicit alternatives.

How each historical decision will be taught

Information available then → stated objective → alternatives → assets, claims, cash and shares changed → risk transferred → later observation.

Keep “management said” separate from “the economic interpretation is.” Later prices cannot prove an earlier motive.

Closed-book learning check

Explain why more BTC need not mean more BTC per share; why a zero coupon has a cost; why a perpetual still has obligations; and how an asset-rich company can face a cash shortfall. Then solve a new version of both examples without the answers.
