# Problem sets A–D

All figures are hypothetical unless a question asks you to retrieve a filing. Units are dollars, except where millions are stated. Show formulas, dates and comparison bases. Use the five-point rubric in the course README. Each set has six questions and a maximum of 30 points.

## Set A. Assets, time and risk

**A1. Reconcile the ledger.** Opening assets are 200, including cash of 30. Debt is 60, preferred stated claims are 40, and common shares are 50. Issue ten common shares at 3, pay issue fees of 2 and retain the remainder. Calculate closing assets, cash, common residual and residual per share. Then pay an 8 preferred distribution. Recalculate and classify the share proceeds, fees and distribution separately.

**A2. Compare the same obligation.** Start with net assets 120 and 60 shares. A 12 preferred distribution must be paid now. Option I: issue six shares at 2.50 and pay it. Option II: pay it from existing assets. No fees. Calculate ending residual per share under both options and compare each with the starting value. Explain why both comparisons are needed.

**A3. Price a bond.** A three-year bond pays 6 at years 1 and 2 and 106 at year 3. Required annual return is 8%. Calculate price, coupon rate and current yield. Explain which metric includes the principal payment.

**A4. Reprice a perpetual.** A hypothetical security pays 10 annually forever, first payment in one year. Calculate price at required returns of 10% and 12.5%. State the percentage price change. Identify two assumptions that make the formula inappropriate for some real preferreds.

**A5. Compound correctly.** An investment rises 40% then falls 30%. Compute total return and geometric annual return. Separately, calculate the real return on an 8% nominal return when inflation is 3%. Explain why subtracting inflation is only an approximation.

**A6. Follow the cash path.** Start with ten BTC and ten cash. Pay 10 at date 1, 20 at date 2 and 20 at date 3, using cash first and then BTC sales. Path A prices: 100, 40, 100. Path B: 100, 160, 100. No taxes, fees or new funding. Compute remaining BTC and its endpoint value for each path. Name the economic reason for the difference.

## Set B. Claims, dilution and options

**B1. Reconstruct a net metric.** BTC value is 460 and USD Assets are 40. Total note principal is 80, of which 20 is in-money with conversion price 5 at the current stock price of 6. Other notes remain out-of-money. Preferred notional is 100 with no conversion. Basic shares are 100; awards add two shares. Treat the in-money note as conversion shares and deduct only out-of-money notes. Calculate net BTC value, fully diluted shares, net value per share and the price multiple. Identify the double count if all 80 of debt is deducted while conversion shares are also added.

**B2. Derive an issuance boundary.** Starting net assets are 120 with 60 common shares. Issue ten shares at 3. Fees are 1. What is ending net assets per share if all net proceeds are retained? How much additional expense/distribution can be paid from those proceeds before that metric falls below its starting value? Ignore other effects.

**B3. Price on proceeds and on claims.** A 100 stated preferred claim is issued for 85. Fees are 1. Annual distribution is 10. Calculate payout/net proceeds, initial change in the stated-claim residual, and the BTC growth needed to make the one-year residual zero after the distribution. Assume all net proceeds buy BTC and the terminal claim remains 100. Explain why this is not a fair-value estimate or perpetual annual hurdle.

**B4. Allocate recovery.** Realizable assets are 180. Debt of 100 ranks first, preferred F of 60 second, preferred J of 40 third, and common last. Compute each layer's recovery. Repeat at assets of 120. State what legal and cost assumptions this simplified waterfall omits.

**B5. Draw a convertible.** Toy note principal is 100; conversion price is 25; no coupon. At maturity, the holder chooses certain cash repayment or four common shares. Calculate payoff at stock prices 10, 25 and 40. At a stock price of 10 before maturity, does conversion parity determine option value? Name two terms needed to apply this diagram to a real note.

**B6. Separate the financing components.** A hypothetical four-year zero-coupon convertible is issued for 100. Under a simplified decomposition, comparable straight debt requires 6%. Calculate the straight-debt present value and the residual attributed to the option. Issue fees are 2. Calculate issuer net cash. Explain why this is an illustrative decomposition rather than a full credit/option model.

## Set C. Treasury operations

**C1. A finite ATM.** Opening cash is 30. Cash charges are 8 in each of four quarters. A 20 put is paid in Q2. Registered common capacity is 20 total; maximum execution is 12 each quarter. No fees. Management raises only the amount needed to cover that quarter's bill, subject to those limits. Show cash, issuance, remaining capacity and unmet need each quarter. Do not hide the shortfall with a plug.

**C2. Read coverage critically.** Cash is 12 million and annual distributions are 6 million, paid 1.5 million per quarter. No financing or BTC sales occur. A 25 million put is due in Q2. Compute the distribution-only coverage in months and the funding gap by the end of Q2. Explain why the first ratio did not describe complete liquidity.

**C3. Cost a rate change.** STRC-like notional is 2,000 million. The board raises the annual rate from 11% to 12.5%. Calculate annual and full-quarter additional cash costs. Does this calculation prove the preferred will trade at stated amount afterward?

**C4. Capacity versus demand.** Average daily volume is two million shares; stock price 100; assumed ATM participation 1%; 60 trading days. Registered capacity is 500 million. Calculate gross volume-limited placement and net proceeds after 1% issue fees. State which constraint binds and what assumption is least established by those figures.

**C5. Translate the euro obligation.** A STRE-like security has EUR 100 million notional and a 10% annual rate. Compute annual USD distributions at EUR/USD of 1.10 and 1.25. Quantify the change. Does holding BTC automatically hedge this EUR/USD cost?

**C6. Cash versus earnings.** During a period, unrealized BTC gains are 40, software operations use three cash, new common raises 15 cash, and preferred distributions consume five cash. No other cash flows occur. What is the cash change? Can you infer complete accounting net income from these facts? Explain the missing bridge.

## Set D. Value and decisions

**D1. Two claim bases.** A perpetual pays 10 annually and has a 100 stated amount. Required return is 12.5%; first payment is one year away. It is issued at its modeled market value. Calculate that value, the initial stated-claim residual change, and the initial market-valued residual change before fees. Then apply fees of 2 to the market-valued residual. Explain the double count if you subtract both its market value and the same distributions' present value.

**D2. Test the premium story.** A memo argues: “Common issuance above a 1.3x asset multiple raises net assets per share; therefore 1.3x is justified forever.” Identify the logical gap. Specify one empirical observation and one counterfactual needed to evaluate a financing-franchise value. A numeric forecast without a reason is not an answer.

**D3. Separate the operating business.** Next three annual software free cash flows are 5, 6 and 7. Terminal operating value at year 3 is 75. Required annual return is 10%. Non-operating treasury assets today are 80; debt is 20; preferred market value is 30; shares are 10. Calculate the operating business value and simplified common value per share. Identify two possible double counts when combining operations and treasury assets.

**D4. Examine a causal assumption.** A model assumes mNAV = 1.20 + 0.50 × LN(BTC price/base BTC price). Calculate mNAV after a 20% and a 70% BTC decline. Explain why four weekly observations cannot establish this as a stable causal law. Name two other financing-demand drivers.

**D5. Investigate incentives.** Management states that a security repurchase reduces future distributions. An analyst claims the real motive is to increase compensation metrics. What evidence would distinguish the claims? Identify who gains a cash claim, who gives up liquidity and which governance documents you would read. Do not invent intent.

**D6. Plan ahead.** Start with cash 10. Quarterly charges are 3 for four quarters, plus a 4 put in Q3. Issuance can raise at most 2 per quarter and 6 total, without fees. Can all payments be funded? Give a feasible schedule if one exists. Compare it with a policy that waits for each cash shortage before issuing. Identify the execution assumption that could invalidate your plan.
