# Assessments

Keep a raw attempt before checking the key. The assessments are original learning exercises, not an accredited qualification. Hypothetical cases use their own simplified rules rather than implying actual Strategy contractual terms.

## Midterm: Units 1–8

Suggested conditions: 90 minutes, closed book, basic calculator and blank paper or blank Excel workbook. No internet, answer key, existing model or assistant. Four questions, 25 points each.

**M1. Transactions and a liquidity problem.** Opening BTC value 150, cash 50, debt 60, preferred stated claims 40, common shares 50. Issue ten common shares at 2.50 with fees of 1. Acquire 30 additional BTC value using cash. The full BTC position then falls 20%. Pay an 8 preferred distribution. Calculate cash, BTC, assets, common residual and residual/share after every step. A 40 debt put is due next; identify the cash gap without assuming financing. Classify the stock issue, acquisition, remeasurement and distribution. Points: ledger 15, per-share calculation 5, classifications and gap 5.

**M2. Payments and price.** Price a two-year bond paying 4 at year 1 and 104 at year 2 using an 8% required return. Separately price a constant annual perpetual payout of 8 at a 10% required return, first payment in one year. Both have 100 stated amount. Explain why coupon, current yield, modeled required return and stated-claim residual are different. Points: calculations 10, distinctions/assumptions 15.

**M3. Financing hurdle.** Issue a 100 preferred stated claim for 90, incur 2 fees and invest net proceeds in BTC. The annual distribution is 10. BTC appreciates 20% over one year. Calculate payout/net proceeds, initial stated-claim residual, one-year residual after the stated claim and distribution, and the BTC return producing zero in that one-year residual. Explain what the result does not tell you about intrinsic value and dividend liquidity. Points: calculations 15, limits 10.

**M4. Security design memo.** A colleague says: “A zero-coupon convertible is free, perpetuals never require cash back, and issuing above 1x means the stock is self-funding.” Correct each claim. Specify the document or calculation needed to evaluate it. Explain how a transaction could improve one metric while worsening another. Points: three corrections 15, evidence 5, coherent counterexample 5.

Gate: at least 80/100, with each critical misconception corrected and one unseen variant solved. A repeated score on the same memorized questions is not an independent pass.

## Final: Units 1–16

Suggested conditions: 120 minutes, blank Excel permitted. Formula sheet limited to one page you wrote yourself. No answer key or assistant. Four questions, 25 points each.

**F1. Capacity does not fix a timing problem.** Opening cash 15, quarterly cash charges 4 for four quarters, and a 10 put in Q2. Common issuance can raise at most 3 per quarter and 10 total, no fees. Find the unavoidable funding gap from aggregate resources. Then give the best feasible timed schedule and identify a first shortfall under a policy that waits for shortages before issuing. Points: aggregate 5, dated schedule 15, interpretation 5.

**F2. Three tests, one decision.** A common-funded preferred repurchase increases a company's net-BTC per-share metric under constant-price assumptions. What additional facts are needed to decide its cash consequences and risk-adjusted economic value? Include execution prices, fees, contractual retirement, future payout reduction, the denominator, displaced cash uses and at least one feasible alternative. Points: complete economic bridge 15, counterfactual 5, uncertainty 5.

**F3. Defend a valuation.** A draft values software, adds all reported BTC and cash, deducts debt face, deducts preferred market value and deducts all future preferred distributions again. It then treats a net-BTC premium as another asset. Identify potential double counts and inconsistent bases. Propose a coherent valuation and a separate reconciliation to the issuer KPI. Explain why a possible premium cannot be proved merely by multiplying the result by a chosen number. Points: defects 10, repair 10, limits 5.

**F4. Evidence under pressure.** You must explain a historical preferred launch. You have today's price, a recent management interview and an old pricing release. Design the case study: documents needed, observation date, stated versus inferred motive, investor alternatives, issue discount/fees, distribution risk, and how to avoid hindsight. State one conclusion that remains unknowable from those documents. Points: evidence design 15, counterfactual 5, uncertainty 5.

## Capstone: the treasury committee

Use two parts. The first is a reproducible hypothetical; the second applies your source discipline to actual Strategy documents.

### Part I. A controlled financing decision

All amounts in USD millions except share prices and BTC prices. Opening BTC market value is 1,000: 10,000 BTC at $100,000 per BTC. Cash is 100; debt principal is 200. Debt interest is 2% annually on opening-quarter principal. Cash principal puts are 60 at the end of Q4 and 140 at the end of Q8. Interest continues through the repayment quarter and stops the following quarter for retired principal.

Preferred F has 100 stated notional and 10% annual distributions. Preferred C has 100 notional and a variable annual rate, 12% in the base case. Assume all scheduled distributions in this case are paid quarterly in cash. No software cash contribution, taxes, further financing or other assets/claims are included. Basic common shares are 100 million and the common price is $11. All claims are deducted at stated amount for the teaching per-share measure; none converts.

Consider three actions at the start:

- No new financing.
- Issue $200 million gross common at $11, with a 1% fee.
- Issue $250 million stated new preferred at 80% of stated amount, with a 10% annual rate and fees of 2% of gross proceeds. No conversion or scheduled maturity.

For either financing action, compare retaining all net proceeds in cash with investing all net proceeds in BTC. Include the new preferred distributions if issued. Hold the common issue price fixed only for this initial transaction; do not assume continued market demand.

Base: BTC price stays flat for eight quarters. Stress: BTC drops 50% immediately after the action, stays there through Q4, then returns to its initial price by Q8. C's annual rate becomes 18% for Q5–Q8 in the stress. For intermediate recovery, use a linear quarterly BTC-price path and label it as assumed. You may sell BTC to meet cash needs; show coins sold, taxes assumed zero, and remaining holdings. Do not count BTC sold as both remaining reserve and cash.

Deliver a source-independent model with cash dates, BTC units/value, claims, shares, distributions, fees and unmet needs. Explain which action looks preferable under each test and why the three answers can differ. You may recommend no new financing if you show a feasible funding path or identify the necessary condition; do not force a capital raise merely because the case offers one.

### Part II. Actual-company decision dossier

Choose one real, dated Strategy financing or repurchase decision. Include MSTR, every outstanding note tranche and all five preferreds in an instrument register. Preserve the actual source date and any amendments. Use current data only if it is verified; otherwise state the historical cutoff.

Submit:

1. A two-page committee memo: decision, objective, constraints, two alternatives, downside and what would change your conclusion.
2. An Excel model plus a source-to-cell register and saved input-change evidence.
3. A contract matrix: proceeds, claims, priority, currency, payout rights, conversion, redemption and puts.
4. A valuation bridge separating software, treasury assets, claims and any argued financing-franchise value.
5. A one-page uncertainty log distinguishing missing evidence from model assumptions.
6. A ten-minute oral defense and five minutes of challenges.

Capstone rubric, 100 points: source discipline 20; transaction and cash reconciliation 25; valuation consistency 20; counterfactual/risk analysis 20; clarity and limits 15. A fabricated source, hidden funding plug or double-counted conversion prevents a pass until repaired.

Oral defense asks: why this metric; why this alternative; what finances the next payment; which assumption matters most; what observation would disprove your explanation? An impressive conclusion without traceable workings is not enough.
