# Worked answers: diagnostic and problem sets

Attempt the questions first. These are result anchors and explanations, not a substitute for a dated Excel ledger. Equivalent consistent methods receive credit. [Exam and capstone solutions](solutions-exams.md) are separate.

## Diagnostic

**1.** Residual = 200 − 80 − 40 = **80**. This subtraction does not establish market value; realization costs, omitted obligations and the chosen claim basis matter.

**2.** Cash and assets rise **30**. Common claims rise by the net financing received. Operating revenue from issuing common is **zero**. No fee is specified.

**3.** BTC's carrying/market value increases **25** under the question's remeasurement assumption; cash changes **zero**. Selling the asset, borrowing or issuing a security is a separate transaction.

**4.** 121 / 1.10² = **100**. The rate and payment dates must use compatible periods.

**5.** 8 / 0.10 = **80**. This assumes constant payments forever, first payment in one year, a constant appropriate discount rate, and no separate redemption cash flow. A discretionary, callable or credit-risky preferred needs additional analysis.

**6.** Initially 120 / 60 = **2**. After raising 30, 150 / 70 = **2.142857**. This improves a specified per-share residual under fixed assumptions; it does not prove that investors earned an excess return.

**7.** 9 / 75 = **12% cash carry** on proceeds. The 9% rate uses stated amount as denominator. Fees would reduce net proceeds. Neither ratio alone prices all rights and risks.

**8.** An investor receives a conversion option; common can be diluted. Principal, puts, maturity, fees and cash settlement can impose costs even without a coupon. Two accurately explained channels earn full credit.

**9.** 40 / 10 = **four years of distributions only**. A 35 put in six months changes the cash schedule. With uniform distributions, five is paid during those six months and the put exhausts the remaining 35. There is no positive cushion for later payments. Do not claim four years of complete funding.

**10.** Apply all three tests: a defined per-share metric, dated cash feasibility and risk-adjusted economic value against an alternative. Need transaction prices/fees, claim terms, deployment, shares and a feasible financing policy. No single answer about BTC/share replaces these tests.

Score each diagnostic question out of two: one for the correct result/claim, one for the reasoning or qualification. Record the first attempt separately from corrections.

## Set A

**A1.** Net issuance proceeds = 10 × 3 − 2 = **28**. Assets = **228**, cash = **58**, residual = 228 − 60 − 40 = **128**, shares = **60**, residual/share = **2.133333**. After the distribution: assets **220**, cash **50**, residual **120**, residual/share **2**. The raise is financing; fees consume transaction value; the distribution transfers cash to preferred holders and reduces the residual in this teaching model. It is not software operating expense or revenue. Exact financial-statement presentation requires the applicable accounting policy.

**A2.** Start at **2/share**. Issue-and-pay gives (120 + 6 × 2.50 − 12) / 66 = **1.863636**, down **6.8182%**. Pay from existing assets gives 108 / 60 = **1.80**, down **10%**. The issue cushions the cost relative to paying the same bill without issuance, but the combined action still ends below the starting value. An issuance-only comparison answers a different question.

**A3.** Price = 6/1.08 + 6/1.08² + 106/1.08³ = **94.845806**. Coupon rate = **6%**; current yield = 6/94.845806 = **6.326057%**. The 8% modeled yield/discount rate prices both coupons and principal. Current yield omits the principal-price difference and timing. Realized return depends on actual payments and reinvestment/exit assumptions.

**A4.** Values = **100** and **80**; change = **−20%**. Payment discretion, credit deterioration, rate resets, redemption rights and a changing discount rate can invalidate the constant perpetuity model. Name and explain two.

**A5.** Wealth multiplier = 1.40 × 0.70 = **0.98**. Total return = **−2%**; geometric annual return = √0.98 − 1 = **−1.005051%**. Real return = 1.08/1.03 − 1 = **4.854369%**. Subtraction gives 5%, ignoring the denominator effect.

**A6.** Date 1 uses the ten cash and sells no BTC. Path A sells 20/40 = **0.5 BTC**, then 20/100 = **0.2 BTC**; **9.3 BTC**, worth **930**, remain. Path B sells **0.125 BTC**, then **0.2 BTC**; **9.675 BTC**, worth **967.5**, remain. The **37.5** endpoint difference comes from selling different quantities to meet the same dated cash bill. Endpoint price alone loses this information.

## Set B

**B1.** Out-of-money debt = 80 − 20 = **60**. Net metric numerator = 460 + 40 − 60 − 100 = **340**. Conversion shares = 20/5 = **4**; diluted shares = 100 + 2 + 4 = **106**. Net/share = **3.207547**; price multiple = 6/(340/106) = **1.870588×**. Deducting all debt while including the four conversion shares counts the same **20** claim twice. This is the question's KPI convention, not proof of actual settlement or a complete equity valuation.

**B2.** (120 + 30 − 1)/70 = **2.128571**. To preserve the original 2/share, ending residual must be at least 140. Available additional leakage = 149 − 140 = **9**. Total fees plus leakage can be ten. Equality is the boundary; any additional expense beyond nine makes it dilutive to this metric.

**B3.** Net proceeds = 85 − 1 = **84**. Carry = 10/84 = **11.904762%**. Initial stated-claim residual change = 84 − 100 = **−16**. One-year zero-residual condition: 84(1 + g) − 100 − 10 = 0, so g = **30.952381%**. The initial discount matters as well as the distribution. This is a one-year terminal-claim scenario. It does not establish a perpetual return hurdle, fair market liability or available payment cash.

**B4.** At 180: debt **100**, F **60**, J **20**, common **0**. At 120: debt **100**, F **20**, J/common **0**. The question assumes an enforceable strict priority, known realizable assets, no liquidation expenses, taxes, secured subordination, arrears or disputed claims. Actual contractual and insolvency rules determine a real recovery.

**B5.** Payoff = MAX(100, 4 × stock price): **100**, **100** and **160**. At 25 the holder is indifferent in this toy model. Before maturity, 4 × 10 = 40 is conversion parity, not the time value of the option. Actual conversion windows/triggers, issuer calls, holder puts, settlement choice, dividends and credit risk can alter the payoff. The toy cash floor assumes certain repayment.

**B6.** Straight debt PV = 100/1.06⁴ = **79.209366**. Residual option component = **20.790634**. Net issuer cash = **98** after fees. The split depends on an assumed straight-debt discount rate and simple additive decomposition; real credit, exercise and hedging interactions require a richer model. Do not call the option economically free.

## Set C

**C1.** Q1: issue **0**, ending cash **22**, capacity **20**, unmet need **0**. Q2: charges 28, issue **6**, cash **0**, capacity **14**, unmet **0**. Q3: issue **8**, cash **0**, capacity **6**, unmet **0**. Q4: issue **6**, cash **0**, capacity **0**, unmet **2**. Uses total 52; cash plus maximum proceeds total 50. Show unmet need separately rather than reporting a fictitious raise of eight in Q4.

**C2.** Distribution-only coverage = 12/6 × 12 = **24 months**. End-Q1 cash = **10.5 million**. Q2 distributions plus put = **26.5 million**; gap = **16 million**. The ratio ignored the principal date.

**C3.** Additional annual cost = 2,000 × (12.5% − 11%) = **30 million**. A full quarter at the new rate adds **7.5 million**. This prices the payment change; it does not predict demand, spreads, price or subsequent board decisions.

**C4.** Gross execution = 2 million × 100 × 1% × 60 = **120 million**, below the 500 million registered limit. Net = **118.8 million**. The volume assumption binds. It does not establish that investors will absorb this amount at a constant price or that observed volume is available to the issuer.

**C5.** Annual payment = **EUR 10 million**. USD cost = **11 million** at 1.10 and **12.5 million** at 1.25: an increase of **1.5 million**. BTC is not a contractual EUR/USD hedge; any offset depends on uncertain joint returns and the actual funding policy.

**C6.** Cash change = −3 + 15 − 5 = **+7**. The unrealized 40 contributes zero cash. Full net income cannot be computed without the accrual, depreciation, tax, interest and other applicable accounting bridge. Preferred distributions also require correct earnings/common-earnings presentation.

## Set D

**D1.** Modeled value = 10/12.5% = **80**. Initial stated-claim residual change = 80 − 100 = **−20**; market-valued residual change before fees = 80 − 80 = **0**. After fees of two, market-valued residual change = **−2**. Subtracting market value 80 and the same distributions' PV of 80 deducts the same economic claim twice. A stated-claim metric and market-valued residual answer different questions.

**D2.** Accretion conditional on an existing premium does not explain why that premium should exist or persist. Need evidence of investor demand/constraints, future feasible issuance, use of proceeds, costs and benefits relative to other BTC exposures. An observation could be executed issuance quantities/prices and resulting net asset changes. A counterfactual could be the same company without future above-asset issuance, or a feasible direct-BTC exposure with matched financing. Correlation alone does not isolate financing-franchise value.

**D3.** Operating value = 5/1.10 + 6/1.10² + (7 + 75)/1.10³ = **71.111946**. Common value = 71.111946 + 80 − 20 − 30 = **101.111946**; per share = **10.111195**. Avoid including operating cash in both business and treasury value; avoid adding terminal proceeds twice or subtracting both preferred market value and the same distributions. The simple debt figure is an assumed valuation basis, not universally fair value.

**D4.** At −20%: 1.20 + 0.50 LN(0.8) = **1.088428×**. At −70%: 1.20 + 0.50 LN(0.3) = **0.598014×**. These are outputs of an assumed function, not forecasts. Four observations cannot establish a stable causal relation, identify confounders or cover changing regimes. Interest rates, volatility, investor flows, liquidity and new issuance can also affect demand. State the model's domain and avoid extrapolating into nonsensical outputs.

**D5.** Seek the contemporaneous authorization, executed repurchase prices/quantities, financing, retired distribution rights, liquidity plan, proxy incentive definitions, board/conflict disclosures and communications. Distinguish cash paid to selling holders from future distributions avoided. Common gains or loses through the full financing/retirement trade, not automatically. Compensation incentives can create a hypothesis; they do not establish private intent. The stated reason and another incentive can coexist.

**D6.** A feasible issuance schedule is **0, 2, 2, 2**. Ending cash is **7, 6, 1, 0**; all 16 of uses are paid from opening ten plus six raised. The reactive schedule issues **0, 0, 2, 2**, leaving unmet needs **0, 0, 1, 1** and two of total capacity unused. Continuing after the first shortfall is a diagnostic schedule, not an assertion that default has no consequences. Prefunding solves the assumed timing constraint, but only if execution actually remains available at the assumed prices and dates.

## Repairing a missed answer

Write your incorrect claim, the first failing step, and a corrected rule. Change at least two inputs and solve again without copying the key. Give an oral explanation of the units and comparison base. Passing an arithmetic variant does not establish knowledge of a real contract; that requires its dated document.
