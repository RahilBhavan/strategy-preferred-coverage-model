Chapter 7

The economics of the machine

Strategy takes one asset, bitcoin, and sells different slices of it to different investors, each at the price that investor will pay. It makes money when the slices sell for more, in total, than the bitcoin behind them costs.

One asset, five kinds of buyer

Different investors want different things from bitcoin. Some want more risk, some less, some want a regular income. Strategy designs a security for each, and each buyer pays for the feature they want.

What the money costs

The hurdle bitcoin has to clear

Here is the cleanest way to see whether the machine works. Total fixed charges are about $1.56B a year. The bitcoin is worth about $69.7B. So bitcoin has to rise about 2.2% a year, measured against the whole stack, just to "earn" the carry. Above that, common holders are ahead. Below it, the carry eats into their value.

For a single new security, the hurdle is its own rate. Raising $1B of STRC at 12% buys about 12,176 BTC at $82,131. The dividend costs $120M a year, about 1,461 BTC at today's price. That trade pays off for common holders only if bitcoin rises faster than about 12% a year over the life of the money. Strategy's bet is that bitcoin's long-run growth will beat its cost of credit by a wide margin. That's a forecast, not a fact, and it's the single assumption the whole model rests on.

Leverage, measured

Net assets are bitcoin ($69.7B) plus cash ($5.5B) minus claims ($20.5B), about $54.7B. A 10% move in bitcoin is $7.0B, which is a 12.7% move in net assets. So at net asset value, common holders own about 1.27x bitcoin. The market premium adds more swing on top. When MSTR trades above its net assets, its price can move further than that 1.27x.

The issuance rulebook

Put the costs together and Strategy's choice of what to sell follows from two prices: the mNAV of the common and the price of the preferreds relative to $100.

The economics in one sentenceStrategy borrows at 0–12% against an asset it believes will grow faster than that, and it sells shares when the market pays more than the assets behind them. Both halves need confidence. One needs bitcoin to rise; the other needs a premium.

Check yourself

Why is the 2.2% hurdle so much lower than STRC's 12%?

The 2.2% spreads all charges across all the bitcoin, most of which was bought with common stock that costs no cash. The 12% applies only to the bitcoin bought with STRC money.

Bitcoin is flat for a year. Where does STRC's dividend come from?

From the USD Reserve or from selling MSTR (if mNAV is above 1.0x). Either way, common holders bear the cost.
