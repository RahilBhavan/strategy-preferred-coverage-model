Chapter 12

The price of each product

Every security is a trade. Strategy gets cash now and gives up something later. The buyer gives cash now and is paid for a specific risk. This chapter prices both sides of every product.

Three numbers do most of the work:

  • Cash raised per $100 of claim: how much money Strategy actually received for each $100 it now owes.
  • Yearly cost on the cash raised: the dividend divided by the cash Strategy actually got. This is the real cost of the money.
  • Break-even bitcoin growth: roughly how fast bitcoin must rise for the trade to help common holders. For a preferred spent on bitcoin, it's about the yearly cost on cash raised.

Each product's trade, both sides

MSTRCommon stock
Strategy gets
Cash with no repayment and no dividend
Strategy pays
A slice of all future value. Selling $1 of stock at net mNAV m gives away $1/m of net assets. At 1.19x that's $0.84 per $1, so existing holders gain $0.16. At 0.8x it's $1.25, so they lose $0.25
Buyer is paid for
Taking the first loss, with leveraged upside (about 1.27x bitcoin at net asset value, Chapter 7)
Economics
The cheapest money when the premium is high, and the most expensive when it's below 1.0x
ConvertsConvertible notes
Strategy gets
Cash at 0–2.25% interest
Strategy pays
Two contingent costs. Dilution if MSTR ends above the conversion price, or cash if it ends below and holders put the notes
Buyer is paid for
Selling interest income in exchange for an option on a very volatile stock. Arbitrage funds hedge by shorting MSTR and earn from the volatility
Economics
Cheap in cash, but the bill is only postponed. The 2029 notes convert at about $672 against an Oct 2026 price near $151, so for now they behave like 0% debt that must be repaid
STRFSenior 10% preferred
Strategy gets
Perpetual money: no repayment, ever
Strategy pays
$10 a year per $100 forever, with a step-up penalty if skipped
Buyer is paid for
Credit risk at the safest preferred rank, with about 88% bitcoin drawdown room before its claims exceed the bitcoin (Chapter 21)
Break-even
Bitcoin ≈ +11.8% a year on money raised at $85
STRCVariable-rate preferred
Strategy gets
A security it can sell continuously near $100, because the rate adjusts
Strategy pays
A rate that rises under stress. The move from 9% to 12% costs about $264M a year at today's ≈ $8.8B. Each 0.25% costs ≈ $22M a year
Buyer is paid for
Credit risk, with less price risk than a fixed-rate preferred. It's a high-yield cash substitute
Break-even
Bitcoin ≈ +12% a year on money raised at $100; 13.3% on the $90 launch money
STREEuro 10% preferred
Strategy gets
Access to European income buyers
Strategy pays
€10 a year per €100, plus currency risk. The euro weakened from $1.1534 at launch to $1.1253, which shrank the claim in dollars from about $894M to $872M. That move helped Strategy
Break-even
Bitcoin ≈ +12.5% a year in euro terms on the €80 launch money
STRK8% convertible preferred
Strategy gets
The lowest preferred rate
Strategy pays
8% plus the conversion option. One STRK converts into 0.1 MSTR, an implied price of $1,000 a share. At $151 MSTR that option is worth about $15 against a $100 face, so today STRK is effectively a plain 8% preferred
Extra lever
It can pay in MSTR shares. Above 1.0x mNAV, that's cheaper than cash in net-asset terms (Chapter 16)
STRDJunior non-cumulative 10%
Strategy gets
A dividend it can stop without owing it later
Strategy pays
The same 10% rate as senior STRF, but sold at $85, so 11.8% on cash. That premium is the price of the option to skip
Buyer is paid for
The most risk: last in line, and payments that can vanish
SoftwareThe operating business
Size
$122.4M of revenue in Q2 2026 with $81.6M gross profit, a 67% gross margin. Annualized revenue is about $490M
Economics
Small next to $1.56B of charges, and its operating cash after costs isn't separately sourced in this book, so the model counts none of it
The patternStrategy pays more cash for money that never has to be repaid. Converts cost almost nothing yearly but come back as puts. Preferreds cost 10–13% on the cash raised, but never mature. Common costs no cash at all, but its true cost swings with the premium. Choosing between them is choosing which risk to hold.
