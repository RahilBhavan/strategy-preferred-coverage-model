Chapter 24

Who decides

Many of the most important numbers in this book are choices, not market outcomes: STRC's rate, whether to sell stock this week, whether to skip a dividend, whether to sell bitcoin. So it matters who makes them.

The people

Michael Saylor co-founded MicroStrategy in 1989, led the move into bitcoin in 2020 and has been Executive Chairman since August 2022. He is also its public voice. Phong Le has been CEO since 2022, after serving as CFO when the strategy began.

Control

Saylor holds Class B shares that carry extra votes per share, giving him roughly a third of the total voting power (Oct 2026 proxy, as reported). That's not a majority, but it means a shareholder vote such as the Oct 28 daily-dividend amendment needs only modest support from other holders to pass.

Why this matters for the analysis

  • Discretion is a risk and a tool. A board that can adjust STRC's rate, pay STRK in shares or pause stock sales can respond to stress. Investors can't predict exactly how it will.
  • Key-person risk. The strategy, the brand and much of the investor following are tied to one person.
  • Aligned incentives. Saylor is a large holder of the common stock, which absorbs losses first. That aligns him with common holders, which isn't the same as aligning him with preferred holders.

Outside referees

Three outside parties shape Strategy's room to move. The SEC requires filings, including the weekly 8-Ks this book relies on. Rating agencies such as S&P (B-, Oct 2025) affect who may buy the securities. Index providers such as MSCI decide whether passive funds must hold MSTR, and froze its index share count growth in Jan 2026.
