Chapter 4

The capital stack: who gets paid first

A capital stack lists everyone with a claim on a company, from first-paid to last-paid. If money runs short, the top gets paid in full before anyone below gets anything. Strategy's stack has seven layers: debt, five preferreds, and common stock at the bottom, which gets whatever is left.

How big is the cushion?

Two things fall out of this picture:

  • Bitcoin would have to fall about 70%, to roughly $24,000, before it was worth only as much as the claims ahead of common. Below that, the common stock is worth nothing on paper.
  • The market values the common at $63.8B, which is more than the $49.2B of bitcoin left after the claims (69.7 − 20.5), or $54.7B counting its $5.5B of cash. That gap is the premium from Chapter 3.
Why seniority matters for the preferredsSTRF sits right under the debt and has a penalty rate if Strategy skips it, so it is the safest preferred. STRD is the most junior, and its dividends can be skipped forever without ever being owed. That's why it pays 10% while STRK, which is more senior and also converts into MSTR, pays 8%.
