The question is how long cash and common issuance can fund preferred dividends and debt interest, and how convertible puts change that answer. The workbook includes STRF, STRC, STRE, STRK and STRD.

Download the Excel workbook. The full model calculates inside Excel. The web calculator below reproduces its snapshot coverage formulas.

What changed in the model

The original 15.2-year figure divides a finite Reserve and ATM authorization by flat annual charges. It assumes enough demand to use that authorization and excludes principal. It is a conditional capacity calculation, not a funding forecast.

The new Quarterly path spans 20 quarters, beginning with an Oct 16 stub. It rolls cash, remaining ATM and shares; stresses BTC and the premium together; charges issuance fees; and includes each put date. It shows unmet financing needs explicitly. After a shortfall, the schedule measures required funding rather than predicting continued solvency.

Net mNAV uses price divided by net bitcoin value per fully diluted share. The bridge moves in-money notes into dilution, deducts out-of-money notes, and deducts preferred claims while adding USD Assets. Snapshot grids hold observed moneyness fixed. The path uses the previous quarter’s price for classification, a disclosed approximation that avoids circular formulas.

Demand scales down with the premium and BTC drawdown. Its coefficients are editable assumptions without historical calibration. A 1x issuance gate is also an assumption. The capital framework describes discretion near 1x, not an unconditional prohibition.

STRC’s optional rate rule

The board’s declared 12% rate is the default. An optional illustration applies February’s monthly VWAP bands using minimum indicated changes. June’s policy subsequently held the rate near par, so enabling the old bands is a stress choice. It is not a forecast of the board’s next action.

Convertible puts

Each note has a conversion price, date and principal. The path defaults to full cash payment at every put, including in-money notes. Moneyness identifies economic pressure; it does not establish conversion eligibility or predict a holder’s election. Partial election assumptions do not refinance the remainder, and all maturities fall beyond this path.

Why can 15.2 years of static coverage coexist with a financing problem?

The static measure treats the ATM as usable and omits principal. A dated path can lose market access before a put or cash payment is due.
