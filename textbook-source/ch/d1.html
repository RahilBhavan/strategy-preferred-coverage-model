Chapter 5

Every big decision, and why

Strategy didn't arrive at five preferreds and a cash reserve by design. Each step solved a problem the previous step created. This chapter walks through fourteen decisions in order: the problem, the options, the reason given, and what it cost.

Each decision separates two kinds of reason. Stated means Strategy said it in a filing or press release, linked in Appendix B. Analysis means my reading of the economics. Strategy didn't necessarily say it.

This chapter tells the story. The economics part that follows puts numbers on every decision: what it cost, what it gained and what the alternative was (Chapter 12 to Chapter 16).

Era 1 · 2020–2021: swapping cash for bitcoin

2020Turn the treasury into bitcoin

Problem: more than $500M of cash in a slow-growing software company, earning little.

Options
Keep the cash; buy back shares; invest in "stocks, bonds, commodities such as gold, digital assets such as bitcoin" (Jul 28, 2020 release)
What it did
Both. A Dutch-auction buyback of up to $250M attracted only $60.5M of shares. On Aug 11 it bought 21,454 BTC for $250M. In Sep 2020 the board adopted bitcoin as its primary treasury reserve asset
Stated
Assets with "potentially higher returns than holding cash" (then-CFO Phong Le). On Aug 11, Saylor cited COVID, quantitative easing and the "depreciating effect on the long-term real value of fiat currencies"
Cost
A volatile, no-income asset replaced a safe one. The company's value became tied to one price
2020–21Borrow with convertibles

Problem: the cash was spent, but the bet needed scale.

What it did
$650M of 0.75% convertibles due 2025 (Dec 2020), then $1.05B at 0% due 2027 (Feb 2021)
Analysis
MSTR's volatility made the conversion option valuable, so buyers accepted almost no interest (Chapter 1, idea 7)
Cost
Dilution if the notes convert; repayment if they don't
2021–22Borrow against the bitcoin (and regret it)

Problem: more leverage, faster.

What it did
$500M of 6.125% senior secured notes (Jun 2021), secured partly by bitcoin. A $205M term loan from Silvergate secured by bitcoin (Mar 2022)
What happened
The 2022 crash took bitcoin to about $15,500. Press reports put the loan's margin-call level near $21,000. Strategy prepaid it in Mar 2023 for about $161M, 22% below face. It redeemed the secured notes in Sep 2024 with convertible proceeds, releasing about 69,080 BTC from the lien
Analysis
The lesson that shaped everything since: keep the bitcoin unpledged. Every later security is unsecured
2022Split the jobs at the top

Problem: a CEO running a software company and a bitcoin strategy at once.

What it did
From Aug 8, 2022, Saylor became Executive Chairman and Phong Le became CEO
Stated
Lets Saylor "focus more on our bitcoin acquisition strategy"
Context
That year Strategy booked $1.29B of bitcoin impairments and a $1.47B net loss under the old accounting rules, which wrote prices down but never back up

Era 2 · 2024–2025: scaling with a premium

2024Sell stock at a premium, borrow at 0%

Problem: none. This was the opportunity. MSTR traded far above its bitcoin, roughly 3.4x on the basic measure in Nov 2024 (a secondary estimate).

What it did
Announced the "21/21 Plan" in Oct 2024: $21B of equity and $21B of fixed income. Sold $3B of 0% convertibles due 2029 at a ~55% conversion premium (conversion price ≈ $672). MSTR joined the Nasdaq-100 in Dec 2024
Analysis
At a high premium, equity is the cheapest money there is. Each dollar of stock buys well over a dollar of bitcoin per share (Chapter 6)
Cost
Reflexivity: the strategy depends on the premium it creates
2025Adopt fair-value accounting

Problem: old rules made bitcoin a one-way loss on the income statement.

What it did
Adopted ASU 2023-08 from 2025: bitcoin is marked to market every quarter
Side effect
Unrealized gains raised the risk of the 15% corporate alternative minimum tax. After Treasury and IRS interim guidance (Notice 2025-49, Sep 30, 2025), Strategy said on Oct 1, 2025 it "no longer expects to become subject to CAMT due to unrealized gains"
Cost
Reported profit now swings by billions with the price, and none of it is cash (Chapter 18)
2025Launch preferred stock

Problem: the "fixed income" half of the plan needed buyers beyond convertible funds.

What it did
STRK (Jan 30: 7.3m shares at $80), STRF (Mar 20: 8.5m at $85), STRD (Jun 5: 11.76m at $85), STRC (Jul 24: 28.0m at $90)
Stated
Part of the 21/21 Plan's fixed income. STRF was pitched as "the pristine fixed income preferred stock" for "the most conservative fixed income investors… to draw a new class of investors into the Bitcoin universe." STRK was "for the bitcoin curious"
Analysis
Perpetual means no refinancing wall. Unsecured keeps the coins free. Unlike debt, a skipped dividend can't force bankruptcy. Strategy didn't state these as reasons
Cost
8–12% a year forever, versus 0% on converts
2025Make one preferred variable (STRC)

Problem: fixed-rate preferreds' prices drift away from $100, so new issuance gets expensive.

What it did
STRC's rate is reset monthly; it started at 9% and reached 12% by Jul 2026. Strategy can also call it at $101 at any time
Stated (Oct 2026 proxy)
The "current intention to maintain STRC Stock's trading price at or close to its stated amount of $100"
Cost
The rate rises under stress (Chapter 11)
Nov 2025Sell in euros (STRE)

Problem: US income buyers are one pool. Europe is another.

What it did
7.75m shares at €80 (upsized from €350M to €620M), listed in Luxembourg
Analysis
New investors, same bitcoin. Selling at €80 gave buyers 12.5% on their cost
Cost
Currency risk and no euro reserve

Era 3 · late 2025–2026: defending the credit

Dec 2025Hold dollars on purpose (USD Reserve)

Problem: with the premium shrinking, "we'll sell stock to pay dividends" stopped being reassuring.

What it did
Set aside $1.44B for dividends and interest. It reached $4.88B by Oct 2026
Analysis
Turns a promise into a balance investors can check every week
Cost
Cash that isn't buying bitcoin
Jun 2026Write the rules down (capital framework)

Problem: STRC traded in the high $80s; investors wanted clear policies.

What it did
Reserve floor of 12 months of charges; STRC to 12%; a $1.0B preferred buyback (raised to $2.0B in Sep) with STRC first; a $1.0B MSTR buyback; the BTC Monetization Program
Stated
To strengthen the preferreds, "enhance liquidity" and "preserve long-term Bitcoin exposure." STRC is the buyback priority "if management determines that repurchases are accretive and would strengthen the Company's capital structure"
Jul 2026Redefine mNAV as "net"

Problem: the old enterprise-value measure no longer described a balance sheet now full of preferreds.

Stated (via press)
"As Digital Credit becomes a larger portion of our balance sheet, we've sharpened our precision"
Critics
The change came weeks after the old measure fell below 1.0x
Analysis
Both can be true. Use whichever version you like, but always label it (Chapter 19)
Jul–Oct 2026Sell common to retire STRC

Problem: STRC below $100 and MSTR above its net assets.

What it did
About 17.0m STRC shares retired for $1.65B by Oct 11, funded by MSTR sales
Analysis
Adds net bitcoin per share while net mNAV > STRC price ÷ $100 (dat-accretion, Chapter 28), and shrinks the dividend bill
Cost
Bitcoin buying slowed; MSTR holders were diluted to retire a claim
Oct 2026Pay dividends every day

Problem: income buyers compare preferreds with money-market funds that accrue daily.

Stated (proxy)
Five reasons: less waiting to reinvest; better liquidity and trading; price stability (for STRC, keeping it near $100); easier access to preferred capital, which benefits common holders; and room for possible continuous trading in future
Status
Shareholder vote Oct 28; STRC first (Nov 2), STRF/STRK/STRD from Jan 4, 2027. Rates and totals unchanged
The thread through all fourteenEach era fixed the last one's weak point. Cash earned nothing, so it became bitcoin. Bitcoin needed scale, so came debt. Secured debt nearly bit, so everything after was unsecured. Equity was cheapest at a premium, so they sold stock. Income buyers were untapped, so came preferreds. Preferred prices drifted, so STRC floats. Dividends needed a credible source, so came the Reserve. The premium shrank, so came buybacks, rules and a new way to measure it.

Check yourself

What did the 2022 Silvergate episode teach, and where do you see it today?

That bitcoin pledged as collateral can force a sale at the worst moment. Every security since is unsecured, and the preferreds have no claim on the coins.

Why sell preferreds when 0% convertibles were available?

Convertibles mature or can be put back, and they depend on a buyer pool of arbitrage funds. Preferreds reach income investors and never mature. Strategy's stated reason was building the "fixed income" side of its plan; the refinancing point is analysis.
